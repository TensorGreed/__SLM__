## Section 1 — Cold open, the Overview

**On screen**: the Overview; spotlight the hero (where you are / next step), then the Data, Train, Results and Ship sidebar entries.

> Welcome to the PII Detector walkthrough. This sample is a span-level
> entity detector — you give it a snippet of text, and it returns a
> structured list of every personal-information span it finds. Email,
> phone, social security, credit card — ten entity types in total.
> This is the Overview: where you are, the next step, and the four
> places we'll visit — Data, Train, Results and Ship. There's a
> confusion risk we'll clear up in a minute.

## Section 2 — Data · Sources

**On screen**: `/project/<id>/data`; open Ingested documents and expand the first row (`expand-doc-*`).

> Data, Sources. Sixty-one source rows. Each row has two columns: the
> text, and a structured list of every entity in that text. The entity
> list is the ground truth — that's the shape the model has to learn
> to produce. Open the ingested documents and expand a row to see what
> one looks like.

## Section 3 — Data · Prepare (disambiguation)

**On screen**: click the **Prepare** step; the redaction options are visible.

> Now the confusing part. Prepare, the cleaning step, has a personal-
> information redaction option. That's a regex pre-processing step —
> it can mask personal information in source text before training. The
> PII Detector model in this sample is the opposite — it finds
> personal information and returns a structured list. Same word in the
> product name, two completely different features. We're not running
> the redaction here.

## Section 4 — Data · Labels

**On screen**: click **Labels**; the labelling-conventions card, then the answer key entries header.

> Labels. This is the answer key — two hundred entries. Each one has a
> snippet, an expected entity list, and a rationale. Above it, the
> labelling conventions: the entity types and a one-line definition of
> each, so every row is labelled the same way. Scoring compares the
> model's predicted entities against the answer key, per entity type.

## Section 5 — Data · Generate

**On screen**: click **Generate**; span generation mode.

> Generate. The lever to grow sixty-one source rows into two thousand
> training rows. For this sample, the generator runs in span mode —
> you list the ten entity types you care about, and the generator
> model writes new text with matching entity annotations. Local Ollama
> is wired up, but we're not running generation here. It's runtime-
> heavy and lives in its own walkthrough.

## Section 6 — Data · Split

**On screen**: click **Split**; the split form and the keep-test-examples option.

> Split. The task shape here is span extraction, and that's the
> contract with scoring: the model has to return an entities array,
> and the result is scored per entity type. The split gives forty-five
> training rows, eight for validation and eight test examples. There's
> also an option to keep the current test examples when you add data,
> so the answer never leaks into training.

## Section 7 — Train · More options · Tokenization

**On screen**: click Train; open **More options** then the Tokenization section.

> Now Train. Under More options, Tokenization. It reports per-row
> token counts and the maximum sequence length you'd budget for, so
> you can see truncation before you spend time training. Surface only
> for this video.

## Section 8 — Train · the form and LoRA

**On screen**: the Train form (base model, task shape); open the LoRA and advanced training section.

> The Train form itself. The base model, the task shape — read-only
> here, span extraction — the number of seeds, a readiness line, and
> one Train button. Under More options, LoRA and advanced training.
> For span extraction the docs recommend raising low-rank adaptation
> from rank eight to rank sixteen, and targeting all four attention
> projections instead of two. Defaults work if you're starting out.

## Section 9 — Results + wrap

**On screen**: `/project/<id>/results`; the four tabs, then **Failures** with the built-in checks.

> Results. Nothing to judge until a run has finished. The page has
> four tabs — Verdict, Failures, Compare and Try it. On Failures
> you'll find the built-in checks, and for this sample the scoring is
> per entity type — precision and recall for email, phone, social
> security, and the other seven. Next video: the sentiment classifier
> sample. Different task shape, different scoring.

## Optional technical notes (background; not spoken)

- `output_schema.scoring_mode = span_set` is the prepared manifest's
  distinguishing field — that's what dispatches the eval handler
  to span-level precision/recall scoring.
- Recommended PEFT settings for span tasks (from
  [slm-docs/docs/demos/pii-detector.md](../../../slm-docs/docs/demos/pii-detector.md)):
  LoRA rank 16, target modules `q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj`.
  Spoken in plain English as "rank sixteen, all four attention
  projections."
- All 10 entity types: email, phone, ssn, credit_card, person_name,
  street_address, date_of_birth, ip_address, api_key, bank_account.
- Gold counts skew toward person_name (138) and email (72); api_key
  is rarest at 21 rows. Eval weights per-class recall, so the rare
  classes still get measured.
