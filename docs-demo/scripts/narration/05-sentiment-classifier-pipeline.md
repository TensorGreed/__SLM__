## Pre-roll (not narrated)

Playwright logs in as admin, clicks the **Demo · Sentiment classifier** tile and lands on the project Overview (`/project/<id>`).


## Section 1 — Cold open, the Overview

**On screen**: the Overview; hero, then the four sidebar entries.

> Welcome to the Sentiment Classifier walkthrough. This is the
> simplest of the three samples — three-way classification with the
> labels positive, neutral, and negative. Thirty source rows,
> perfectly balanced ten, ten, ten. Each row is text and a single
> label. We start on the Overview, then visit Data, Train, Results and
> Ship.

## Section 2 — Data · Sources

**On screen**: open Ingested documents and expand the first row.

> Data, Sources. Thirty source reviews. Each row has two columns: the
> text, and the label. Expand one. You see exactly the shape the model
> has to learn — read a review, emit one of three labels. The balance
> matters: ten of each class means the model never gets to cheat by
> always predicting the majority.

## Section 3 — Data · Labels

**On screen**: answer key entries header and the mix summary (70/65/65).

> Labels. The answer key has two hundred entries, and the mix summary
> shows the distribution — seventy positive, sixty-five neutral,
> sixty-five negative. Slightly skewed positive, typical of real-world
> reviews. Scoring measures per-class precision and recall against
> this answer key, so under-represented classes still get measured.

## Section 4 — Data · Split

**On screen**: the split form and the keep-test-examples option.

> Split. The task shape is classification with a text column and a
> label column, and every prepared row is canonicalised to that. The
> split gives twenty-two training rows, four for validation and four
> test examples. Small, but enough to verify the loop end to end.

## Section 5 — Train · More options · Tokenization

**On screen**: Train, **More options**, Tokenization section.

> On to Train. Under More options, Tokenization. The twist for this
> sample: the target profile is mobile CPU, so maximum sequence length
> matters more than usual. Short sequences mean a smaller model
> footprint and faster inference on-device.

## Section 6 — Train · the form and LoRA

**On screen**: the Train form; LoRA and advanced training section.

> The Train form. Base model, task shape, seeds, readiness, and one
> Train button. Under More options, LoRA and advanced training. The
> project's mobile CPU target hints at smaller batches, shorter
> sequences, and a tighter model footprint on export. The defaults are
> tuned for it.

## Section 7 — Results

**On screen**: `/project/<id>/results`, Verdict tab.

> Results. Empty until we have a finished run. For this sample the
> checks are the classification defaults — accuracy and macro-F1 in
> the headline, per-class precision and recall in the detail. The
> Verdict tab is where they land.

## Section 8 — Ship · Package

**On screen**: `/project/<id>/ship`; Options, **Compress first**, then the export form.

> Ship, Package. Under Options, compress first, then the export form.
> The natural target for this sample is ONNX with eight-bit
> quantization, which would give us a fast on-device model. ONNX is in
> the export format list, but the end-to-end story for this sample
> isn't validated yet — that's Video Eleven. For now we're just
> confirming the shape of the export surface.

## Section 9 — Wrap

## Things to **not** say

- Don't claim the ONNX-INT8 export *has* been validated — it
  hasn't. Say "the natural target" or "in the export format list",
  not "it works."
- Don't speak the manifest's `target_profile = mobile_cpu`
  literal. Say "the mobile CPU target profile" in plain English.
- Don't list per-class gold counts beyond what's said (70/65/65) —
  reading more numbers aloud feels recital-style and the on-screen
  display covers the detail.

  adapter literal strings) — same rule as the other videos.

## Optional technical notes (background; not spoken)

- Eval pack id: `evalpack.classification.default`. Configured via
  the manifest's `evaluation.preferred_pack_id`. Dispatches to the
  classification handler in
  [backend/app/services/eval_task_handler_service.py](../../../backend/app/services/eval_task_handler_service.py).
- ONNX export is in the export format enum at
  [backend/app/models/export.py](../../../backend/app/models/export.py)
  but no successful export run has been observed in this pass. Open
  question Q23 / Q24 — parked here, answered by Video 11.
- Source distribution is exact 10/10/10 by construction; gold
  70/65/65 reflects the real-world skew toward positive reviews
  even when curating.
- Q12 (classification-specific synthetic path) is still open —
  the synthetic tab is intentionally skipped in this video to avoid
  showing an unverified surface.

## Why no synthetic section

Videos 03 and 04 each included a Synthetic tab section. This video
skips Synthetic because Q12 — "is there a classification-specific
synthetic data generation path?" — remains unresolved. The Synthetic
tab does render for this sample but the surface looks generic; the
recording would either invent a story or have to explain a caveat
that distracts from the class-balance / mobile-target beats.
Compression and Export get the freed time instead.
